# Brief reader — five-frame storyboard

Status: review prototype. Actual completed brief not available in this workspace. Presentation excerpts are not a substitute for the full source.

## 01. From the whole to the brief.

The assembled object becomes a document at the same visual anchor. The left navigation stays in place; the reader now controls the pace.

Interaction: Keep orientation through the handover. The drawing can fade as the document appears; no zoom, forced rotation or new scroll lock.

Source: Presentation pp. 2, 4–5: consistent layout and student-facing language.

Needed: Complete brief title, module and assessment information.

## 02. Find the essentials first.

Give the task, submission requirements and deadline a dependable place. Keep these visible when supporting detail is closed.

Interaction: The task remains readable without opening anything. Supporting material sits below it; the deadline is never hidden in an accordion.

Source: Presentation pp. 6, 13. W3C: clear page structure.

Needed: Confirmed deadline, weighting, deliverable formats and any submission conditions.

## 03. See what connects.

Show the relationship between the work students undertake, the learning they demonstrate and the criteria used to assess it.

Interaction: In the finished reader, selecting a task emphasises its related outcomes and criteria. All wording stays available; colour is not the only indication of a relationship.

Source: Presentation p. 2: alignment between outcomes, tasks and criteria. The presentation does not supply the full mapping.

Needed: Exact learning outcomes, task requirements and rubric. Map only relationships supported by that content.

## 04. Open only the detail you need.

Group requirements, context and resources under descriptive headings. Let readers open more than one group and keep their place.

Interaction: Native expandable sections work by pointer, touch and keyboard. Opening one does not close another. Scrolling never changes the reader’s choices.

Source: Presentation pp. 6, 11. CAST UDL: organise information and resources.

Needed: Complete guidance and resource links. Preserve essential requirements even when detail is collapsed.

## 05. Turn requirements into next steps.

Bring the schedule, checklist and self-assessment together, so readers can plan their work and return to the criteria.

Interaction: Checkmarks record preparation, not grades. Keep the submission instructions beside the checklist. The reader can return to any section without replaying the story.

Source: Presentation pp. 10, 13–14. CAST UDL: planning and monitoring progress.

Needed: Confirmed schedule, submission method, complete checklist and self-assessment wording.

## Content integrity

The presentation demonstrates features and rationale, not measured effectiveness. Do not invent outcomes, dates, weights, criterion mappings or submission requirements. Confirm all transcribed excerpts against the completed brief before building the live reader.

## Access and evaluation

Normal scrolling, real anchor links, keyboard-operable controls, multiple open groups, no interaction gates. All-visible content route required for the finished reader. Test task-finding, understanding of requirements, navigation, zoom and assistive technology with readers. Checklist completion is preparation, not assessment achievement.
