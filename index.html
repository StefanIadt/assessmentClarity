Neurodivergent students may encounter barriers in language, visual presentation, attention and processing. These experiences differ from person to person. The visual examples above explore barriers created by information design; they do not reproduce or diagnose a condition.

01 / LANGUAGE

Say what
you mean.

Use direct language. Explain unfamiliar terms. Pair abstract criteria with concrete examples.

02 / CONSISTENCY

A familiar
way through.

Use consistent headings and terms across modules. Give students a reliable place to find the task, criteria and deadline.

03 / STRUCTURE

Give each thing
its place.

Break long briefs into meaningful sections. Separate the task, deliverables and assessment criteria.

04 / ACCESS

More than
one way in.

Provide accessible text, flexible presentation and readable alternatives. Let students control how they engage.

AN EXAMPLE / CRITICAL REFLECTION

What am I
being asked to do?

Explain how your research shaped your design. Use examples from your work. Describe what you changed, why you changed it, and what you learned.
