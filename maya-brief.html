Brief Overview

Choose two features in an existing digital product—for example, search and filtering, onboarding, or notifications. Investigate how psychological principles help explain the experience. Compare published evidence, analyse the features and justify improvements.

Your group submits shared work. Each student keeps their own record of decisions, contribution, feedback and learning.

These are illustrative assessment requirements, not a live IADT brief.

Literature Review

What to investigate

Relate your two selected features to attention, cognitive load, mental models and recognition versus recall. Select the principles that help explain your case; do not force every principle into the analysis.

How to review the literature

Compare evidence, rather than summarise one source at a time.+

Organise the review around questions or themes. Compare methods, findings, limitations and disagreements. Explain how the evidence informs your analysis of the features.

Use a clear evidence base.+

Include at least six relevant peer-reviewed articles. Books can establish concepts. Podcasts, videos and professional articles can provide examples and debate, but do not replace the research evidence.

Make the requirements visible.+

Write 2,000 words (±10%) for the group review. Use APA 7 citations and references. Exclude the reference list and appendices from the count. The individual reflection is a separate 500-word submission.

Read the transcript+

Welcome to Psychology of UX. Work in a group to compare two interface features using psychological principles and research. Each student also keeps an individual reflective journal.

Your group literature review is two thousand words, plus or minus ten percent. Use APA 7 referencing and at least six peer reviewed articles. The reference list and appendices do not count towards the word limit.

Use the resources to read, listen and watch. Library links take you to IADT services, where you may need to sign in. Evaluate the research evidence rather than treating every resource as equal.

Keep your own record of contributions, decisions and feedback. Reflect on your performance and your next step. Your individual critical reflection is five hundred words. The group shares its output, but each person explains their own learning.

The rubric separates the group research and analysis from individual reflection and contribution. You can review a personal log alongside the individual criterion. Formative feedback helps you identify what to improve before submission.
