ASSESSMENT BRIEFILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

Evaluating a claim.

A short fictional task showing the reader’s structure. It does not reproduce the live assessment or save your responses.

01 / Orientation

Where do I begin?

The task
Choose a claim relevant to your subject. Compare the evidence and explain your conclusion.
What to submit
A reasoned response, a source list and a short reflection.
When
Stage 1: evidence review, week 3.
Stage 2: response and reflection, week 6.

A summary keeps the essentials together. Why clear structure matters ↗

02 / Manageable detail

What is expected?

Your response should reach a conclusion that is supported by evidence. Open the guidance you need; the task and submission requirements remain above.

Choosing and comparing evidence

Select relevant sources, explain their credibility, and consider evidence that challenges your initial view.

Preparing your response

State the claim, compare the evidence, and explain how it supports your conclusion. Identify any important limitations.

Reflecting on your learning

Explain one way your understanding changed and identify the evidence that prompted that change.

Related details stay in meaningful groups. You control what opens. Design rationale ↗

03 / Alignment and self-assessment

How will my work be assessed?

Learning

Evaluate evidence to reach a reasoned conclusion.

Evidence

Your comparison of sources and the conclusion in your response.

Criterion

The quality of your evaluation and how well it supports your conclusion.

Compare the descriptions for this example criterion.

Select a description to see how the same criterion distinguishes the quality of evidence.

The tool’s self-assessment uses the assessor’s rubric. Reviewing a description helps make expectations explicit; selecting a band does not determine a final grade.

04 / Reader choice

Can I read this my way?

The working brief includes searchable contents, focus, display, contrast and audio controls. Educators can also attach a recorded explanation.

This demonstrates one reading adjustment. Audio and walkthrough playback are not simulated here. The same essential information should remain available in the written brief.

05 / Checking and reflection

Am I ready to submit?

Use the requirements to review your preparation, then return to the criterion to judge the quality of your evidence.

Example submission checklist

0 of 3 preparation checks complete

Completion and achievement are different questions. A checklist helps prepare a submission; the rubric supports judgement of its quality.

The project / what supports the approach

Design decisions.
Made explicit.

What has been built

A working brief builder with configurable sections, rubrics, schedules and student-facing reading and reflection tools.

The example above demonstrates selected behaviours. It is not the production application.

What informs it

W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance and CAST UDL inform consistent organisation, meaningful grouping and support for reviewing progress.

W3C: consistent design ↗

CAST: monitoring progress ↗

What needs evaluating

Whether students can find requirements, explain expectations and judge their evidence more confidently and accurately.

Those are evaluation questions. This prototype makes no claim of measured improvement.
