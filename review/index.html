The proposed mobile flow

Read a story chapter → Open its brief section → Read and inspect → Back to the same chapter.

Vertical gestures scroll the page. A sideways swipe on the persona cards changes person. Chapter changes are deliberate: use the chapter selector or Previous / Next. The brief opens full height inside the same page; its own contents scroll naturally, and its accessibility controls remain at the bottom. The story does not advance while someone is reading.

Design decision for review: mobile separates storytelling from close reading. Desktop retains the paired story and brief. This is a prototype of that change, rather than a production release.

What was causing inconsistent scrolling?

FindingConsequencePrototype correction
Touch movement was cancelled and forwarded as pixel deltas between two frames and the page.Native momentum was lost. Gestures could change the story while the reader expected to move the brief.Mobile uses native page, card and reader scrolling. No manual touch forwarding on coarse-pointer mobile screens.
Wheel deltas in pixels, lines and pages were treated as the same unit vertically.Mouse wheels and trackpads could move the story at very different rates.Normalize forwarded wheel values: 16px per line, one frame viewport per page. Leave native reader scrolling to the browser.
The parent journey used an arbitrary 0.8 × window-height chapter distance.Story movement and embedded-frame movement had different scales.Desktop chapter distance now matches the actual case viewport, giving a one-to-one scroll distance.
Maya’s overview programmatically jumped to a later task row.The mobile reader could open already scrolled down.On mobile, start at the top. The desktop task alignment remains.

Native momentum should remain device-specific; making touch and mouse movement identical by forcing one fixed speed would make the experience less predictable. The goal is consistent ownership and distance, while preserving each device’s familiar behaviour.

Controls and themes

The original brief controls are retained. Audio and incidental demo actions stay disabled. Student Actions opens beside its trigger; on mobile it sits above the bottom toolbar. The master theme applies to the story and the reader.

What has been checked

Chromium mobile touch emulation at 390 × 844, narrow layout at 320 × 740, orientation changes, and desktop at 1440 × 900. Every Sam and Maya chapter was opened and captured. Scrollable reader sections accepted touch movement; short sections needed no scroll. Native persona swiping, outer-page isolation while reading, return-position restoration, and no horizontal page overflow were checked.

Forwarded desktop wheel test: 16 pixel units → 16px; 1 line → 16px; 1 page → the case viewport height. No JavaScript errors were recorded in the chapter walkthrough.

Still to validate together: the mobile story-to-reader transition, real iPhone Safari momentum and toolbar resizing, and real Android touch behaviour. Emulation cannot prove the feel of a physical device. Desktop’s remaining pinned visual sections are retained for comparison.
