Project Details

Project Overview

Create a responsive online guide for a public audience. Build it, test it and explain your implementation decisions.

Choose an everyday topic you can explain clearly to someone unfamiliar with it. Decide what that reader needs to find, understand and do.

Develop the guide across six teaching weeks. Use an early checkpoint to test your structure before completing the website.

Two opportunities to review:

  • Week 3: discuss an early structure, sample content and a test plan.
  • Week 6: submit the final site, supporting evidence and reflection.

The assessment is individual. You may exchange feedback, but your implementation, evidence and reflection must show your own decisions.

Learning Outcomes Assessed

① Apply relevant methods and explain the evidence behind your decisions.

② Evaluate your work and identify how it could improve.

Key Expectations

Show the evidence behind your decisions.+–

Keep a record of the sources, methods or tests that inform your work. Explain why they are appropriate to the task.

Review and improve your work.+–

Use feedback and the criteria to identify a useful change. Record what you changed and why.

Deliverables

1. Working website+–

A public URL for the final website, with content that can be read and navigated on a phone or laptop.

  • Organise the guide with clear headings and meaningful navigation.
  • Use content and images you have permission to publish.
  • Check the experience with keyboard navigation and at different screen widths.
  • Explain any remaining limitations rather than hiding them.
2. Implementation and testing evidence+–

Include a repository link and a short record of the decisions you made. Identify what you tested, what you found and what changed.

A concise sequence of screenshots and notes is sufficient. Select evidence that helps another person understand your reasoning.

3. Reflection+–

Explain one important decision, the evidence behind it and one improvement you would make next. Relate your judgement to the rubric rather than only describing the steps you completed.

4. Submission and link checks+–

Provide the site URL, repository URL and your evidence and reflection files. Check that each can be opened without using your account.

A complete submission still needs to demonstrate the quality described in the rubric.

Schedule

Week 3 checkpoint

Bring an early structure, a sample page and your proposed tests. Use feedback to decide what needs attention before completing the website.

Frequently Asked Questions

Finding an answer

Where should I start?+–

Read the overview and deliverables. Identify the first small action: choose a topic and outline what a reader needs to know. You can return to the complete requirements throughout the project.

What if I cannot finish every planned feature?+–

Make the core reading experience work, identify what is incomplete, and explain the limitations in your evidence and reflection. Use the published rubric to judge the implications.

Can I use a different technical approach?+–

For a live brief, discuss proposed changes with the lecturer. In this example, the important point is to make the reasoning and evidence visible rather than assume every tool is equally appropriate.

What is the difference between the checklist and rubric?+–

The checklist helps you confirm required items are present. The rubric describes the quality that the evidence should demonstrate.

How Work is Marked

What is looked at

Your evidence is judged against the published criteria and grade descriptions. A complete set of files is necessary, but it does not establish their quality.
